# `systems-biology-learning-path` prompts

Learn or teach systems biology with a plan that fits your background, practice with feedback, and the
workbook's prioritised checklist.

## 1. Study plan

> I want to learn systems biology. I'm a biologist with school-level maths and have 6 weeks, about 8 hours per week. Make me a study plan.

A good answer: a short diagnostic, then the four parts in order with priority 1 topics first (matrix
operations, rank, eigenvalues; stoichiometric matrix and steady state; ODEs, stability and feedback;
balances and Michaelis–Menten). Most of the time goes to solving problems rather than reading, with
attempts before solutions and a mistakes log. Each topic aims at declarative, procedural, contextual and
conceptual knowledge.

## 2. Explain to a biologist

> Explain eigenvalues to a biologist using a two-species model.

A good answer: an eigenvector is a direction of change that the system only stretches, and its eigenvalue
is the growth or decay rate along that direction. For a Jacobian, negative real parts mean perturbations
die out, and complex values mean damped oscillation. It gives an example such as rows (0, 1) and
(−2, −3) with eigenvalues −2 and −1.

## 3. Quiz me

> Quiz me on stoichiometric matrices, then check my answers.

A good answer: problems at increasing difficulty (build N, find the rank, the null space, conservation
relations), each answered by the learner first, then checked with the scripts. Feedback names the
misconception (for example balancing external metabolites).

## 4. How to study the maths

> I keep reading the solutions and then forgetting how to do the problems. How should I study the maths?

A good answer: attempt each problem before looking at the solution, ideally waiting a day, and iterate
between problems and theory. Practise the specific weakness deliberately, connect new ideas to what you
already know, work in a group of 3 to 5 at a similar level after trying alone, and expect the early phase
to be slow.

## 5. Design a flipped classroom

> Design a two-week flipped-classroom course on introductory systems biology for master's students from mixed backgrounds.

A good answer: preparation before each session (reading and short videos with stated objectives), class time
for problem solving in small groups and discussion, the four parts with priorities, clear objectives per
session, and feedback on practice. It addresses the different backgrounds: maths practice for
biologists and biological detail for mathematicians.
