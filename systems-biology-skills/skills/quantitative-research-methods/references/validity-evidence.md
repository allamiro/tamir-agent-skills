# Validity evidence

Validity is a property of an interpretation and use of scores, argued from several kinds of evidence. The
grouping below is the widely used five-source framework (content, response processes, internal structure,
relations to other variables, consequences); author knowledge, VERIFY against the current Standards for
Educational and Psychological Testing.

| Source | Question | Evidence | Biology analogue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Content | Do items cover the construct map? | Map-to-item table, expert review | Do the readouts cover the pathway states of interest? |
| Response processes | Do respondents answer for the intended reasons? | Think-alouds, exit interviews | Does the reporter respond to the pathway, not to cell size or stress? |
| Internal structure | Do items behave as the model expects? | Fit, dimensionality, Q3, reliability, invariance across groups | Do markers of one state co-vary? |
| Relations to other variables | Do scores relate to what they should and not to what they should not? | Convergent and discriminant correlations, prediction | Does the score predict an independent functional readout? |
| Consequences | What happens when scores are used, intended and not? | Follow-up of decisions, fairness checks | Does a biomarker cut-off mis-assign a subgroup to treatment? |

## Reliability in brief

- Internal consistency (Cronbach's alpha) applies to reflective scales; it rises with the number of items.
- Spearman–Brown predicts reliability at a different test length: ρ' = f ρ / (1 + (f − 1) ρ).
- Test–retest and inter-rater agreement answer different questions; report the one that matches the use.
- Reliability needed for decisions about individuals is higher than for comparing group means.

## Interpretation with consequences (after Hattie's foreword, paraphrased)

Ask four questions before acting on a result: was the method appropriate, were the decisions made during the
analysis sound and reported, is the interpretation defensible from the evidence, and are the data of
adequate quality? Then state what decision the result supports and what happens if it is wrong.
