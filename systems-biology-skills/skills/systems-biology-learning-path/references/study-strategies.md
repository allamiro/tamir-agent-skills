# Study strategies (paraphrased from the workbook's introduction)

## What the workbook stresses, with the effect sizes it reports (Cohen's d)

| Factor | d as reported | Practical meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Belief that one can succeed (self-efficacy) | 1.81 | Set attainable goals and record progress |
| Clear learning objectives | 1.12 | State what each session should achieve |
| Deliberate practice objectives | 1.13 | Practise the specific weakness, not what is comfortable |
| Regulating one's own effort | 0.75 | Plan time; persist through the slow phase |
| Elaboration done by the learner | 0.7 (vs 0.44 when the teacher does it) | Connect new ideas to known ones yourself |
| Group work | 0.68 | Groups of 3 to 5 of similar level; try alone first |
| Embracing challenges and mistakes | 0.44 overall; worse during learning, better after | Errors now, better transfer later |

Reported from Schneider and Mustafić (2015) via the workbook; read the primary source before quoting.

## Working pattern for mathematical material

1. Skim the notes calmly.
2. Look at the problems and see what you can already solve.
3. Study the worked examples.
4. Go back to the explanations and textbooks with the problems in mind.
5. Iterate between problems and theory.
6. At the end, make sure you understand all of it; look at solutions only after a real attempt, ideally a day later.

## Background-specific advice

- **From biology:** mathematics needs few facts but much practice; expect slow progress at first.
- **From mathematics, physics, engineering:** biology needs a large factual base; models fail without it.
- **From computing:** check what a tool assumes before using its output; reuse libraries, but understand them.
- **Everyone:** biologists often face inverse problems (inferring causes from observations), while engineers
  build systems forward from parts; naming the difference helps teams communicate.

## Videos and notes

Set one or two objectives before a video; pause to think; take notes with a question column and a summary
(the Cornell method), completed within a day; watch in short sessions.
