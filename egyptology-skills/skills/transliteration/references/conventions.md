# Transliteration conventions

## Letters used (current Egyptological practice)

ꜣ ꞽ (or i) ꜥ w b p f m n r h ḥ ḫ ẖ s š q k g t ṯ d ḏ. This set is from author knowledge: VERIFY

## Differences to expect in older books

- Gardiner's sign-list prints some letters differently from current style (for example ḳ where
  current writing has q, and a different mark for the first two weak consonants). The exact
  letters differ by edition, and the OCR of the supplied copy mangles them: check the page image.
- When quoting a source, keep its spelling and add the modern form beside it.

## Brackets (Gardiner, p. 441)

Round brackets ( ) have two meanings in the sign-list:

1. A consonant that must be understood but is not written, as in Wꜣḏ(y)t "Edjo".
2. A consonant that is written but had disappeared from the pronunciation, as in sw(r)i "drink".

For verbs with a weak final consonant i (the "3ae inf." class) the unwritten i is added in the
transliteration without brackets (Gardiner's example: pri).

## s and z

Old Egyptian kept s and z apart, which is why some Middle Egyptian words are printed with s (z).
The sign-list explains this for sꜣ "son", once read zꜣ (p. 441).
