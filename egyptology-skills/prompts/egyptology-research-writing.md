# `egyptology-research-writing` prompts

Write up, submit and revise Egyptological work so that a specialist can check every reading, and get it
through review and proofs intact.

- **Give it:** the source and its images with permissions, your reading and its conventions, the question and
  finding, earlier publications, the target journal, the authors, and for revisions the reviews

## 1. Write up a stela

> Help me write up an unpublished Middle Kingdom stela in a regional museum for publication. I have photographs, my transliteration and translation, and two parallels. Outline the article and tell me what I must settle before I write.

A good answer: choose the journal first and read its instructions. Settle the image permissions in writing
and decide authorship and acknowledgements. Then an outline: introduction with the question; the source
(museum number, provenance, description, images and credit, epigraphic method); conventions declared once;
transliteration and translation with restorations marked; commentary; discussion against the parallels;
abstract written last. It keeps reading apart from interpretation.

## 2. Which journal

> I have a new reading of one line of a well-known Coffin Text spell. Which kind of journal should I send it to, and how does that change the article?

A good answer: a philological journal for a short article on the passage, with the evidence for the new
reading and a fair statement of the old one. A broad interpretive venue would need the detail in notes. It
says to read the journal's instructions and recent articles, and to plan a second journal in case of rejection.

## 3. Reviewer disputes a reading

> A reviewer says my reading of a damaged sign as the owl (G17) is impossible and it must be the vulture (G1). How do I respond?

A good answer: a point-by-point response with the sign-level evidence (traces, spacing, parallels,
photographs or a new collation). It concedes if the evidence does not decide between the readings and
marks the reading as uncertain with both options, thanks the reviewer if the point improved the article,
and adds no claim beyond the evidence.

## 4. Rejected

> My article on a private letter was rejected by the first journal. What now?

A good answer: rejection is common and not final. Take the constructive comments, revise, choose the next
journal with any co-authors, and resubmit with a new cover letter. If several journals reject it for the
same reason, ask whether the reading or the argument needs more work.

## 5. Check the proofs

> The proofs of my text edition arrived and I have a week. What should I check?

A good answer: compare the hieroglyphs, diacritics, transliteration, line numbers, plate references and
bracket marks against your own copy, because fonts and conversions corrupt them. Correct errors only, since
major changes are not allowed at proof stage. Check image credits and captions, and reply on time.
