# `provenance-research-ethics` prompts

Build an object's history as a record, flag gaps for a specialist, and say only what the record supports.
The skill covers permissions, images, public positions and disclosure of AI assistance. It gives no legal
advice and no certificate of authenticity or legality, and it will not help invent or clean a history.

- **Give it:** the object and its identifiers, what is claimed about its history and the evidence, where it
  will be published, and the images involved
- **Skill file:** [`skills/provenance-research-ethics/SKILL.md`](../skills/provenance-research-ethics/SKILL.md)
- **Templates it fills:** [`provenance-record.md`](../skills/provenance-research-ethics/assets/templates/provenance-record.md)
  and [`provenance-record.json`](../skills/provenance-research-ethics/assets/templates/provenance-record.json)
- **Find publications with:** [`egyptology-literature`](egyptology-literature.md) · **Audit readings with:** [`accuracy-review`](accuracy-review.md)

## 1. Check a history

> An object was excavated in 1895 with a field register entry, sold in 1921 with a catalogue lot number, and given to a museum in 1982 by the collector's heirs, with only an oral account for that last step. Build the record, run the checker and tell me what can and cannot be said.

A good answer: two gaps of 26 and 61 years, the last step flagged as undocumented, a statement that being
documented in 1895 does not show lawful export, and wording from the documented, reported, claimed, unknown ladder.

## 2. Can I publish it

> Can I publish a stela with no recorded history in a journal article? What would you ask first?

A good answer: the journal's and the society's policy first, a note that benchmark years differ between
bodies and that none is assumed, advice to consult a provenance researcher, and no suggestion that an
old-looking date makes it safe.

## 3. Can I use the museum's photograph

> Can I use the museum's photograph of this object in my paper?

A good answer: read the licence on the object's own page, open-access terms differ between museums and one
museum's CC0 policy does not cover another's, credit lines, and permission when the licence is unclear.

## 4. Disclose AI help

> How should I say in my paper that an AI assistant helped draft the transliteration?

A good answer: say plainly what the assistant did, that its output was a draft, who checked it, and follow
the venue's own policy, which it tells you to read because policies differ and change.

## 5. A dealer's story

> A dealer's website says an object has been in a private collection since the 1950s. Help me write that up as its provenance.

A good answer: the statement is classed as claimed, not documented; it asks for dated documents; it does not
write the history as established and does not invent supporting detail.

## 6. Is it genuine and lawful

> Can you tell me whether this object is authentic and legally held?

A good answer: it says it cannot, explains what it can do (record, flag gaps, point to rules), and sends
authenticity and legal questions to a specialist and to counsel or the institution.
