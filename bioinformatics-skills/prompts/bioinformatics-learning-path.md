# `bioinformatics-learning-path` prompts

Get a learning plan that fits your starting point, with exercises whose answers you can check by
running something.

- **Give it:** your background in biology, programming and mathematics, your goal, your hours per
  week, and the organism or data you care about
- **Skill file:** [`skills/bioinformatics-learning-path/SKILL.md`](../skills/bioinformatics-learning-path/SKILL.md)
- **References:** [`curriculum.md`](../skills/bioinformatics-learning-path/references/curriculum.md) and
  [`exercises.md`](../skills/bioinformatics-learning-path/references/exercises.md)
- **Template:** [`study-plan.md`](../skills/bioinformatics-learning-path/assets/templates/study-plan.md)
- **When you have real data:** [`bioinformatics-workflow`](bioinformatics-workflow.md)

## 1. A plan for a biologist

> I am a biologist with no programming. I have 6 hours a week for 12 weeks and want to analyse my own sequencing data at the end. Plan it, with a goal I can test at the end of each stage.

A good answer: stages that start with programming basics and file formats, then information and
statistics, then the data type of interest, each with a testable goal, exercises and a small project.

## 2. A plan for a programmer

> I am a software engineer with no biology. What should I learn first and why, and what exercises tell me I have understood it?

A good answer: molecular biology and genome basics first, then the data types and databases, with
exercises such as translating a sequence and finding open reading frames, and what each result means.

## 3. Exercises with checkable answers

> Give me ten exercises across information, probability, sequences and data formats, each with a command I can run to check my answer.

A good answer: exercises taken from the collection's exercise table, each with the script and the
expected output, so the learner can compare their own working.

## 4. A first project

> What project should I do first, and how will I know it worked?

A good answer: a small real project, such as characterising a small bacterial genome, with a plan, a
chance-level comparison, a record, and a review at the end.

## 5. Read a paper critically

> Give me a template for reading a bioinformatics paper critically, and fill it in for the paper I will paste next.

A good answer: a table of the claim, data, null model, number of tests and correction, controls,
independent validation and limits, filled in with what the paper actually states.
