# `research-proposal-planning` prompts

Turn an idea into a plan someone else can judge: a question that can fail, aims with success criteria, a
data plan, a schedule with its critical path, a risk list, and agreed credit. The skill also checks a data
management plan for missing elements and a schedule against a deadline.

- **Give it:** the idea, the audience, the time and people available, the data you have or hope to get, and
  any funder or institutional constraints
- **Skill file:** [`skills/research-proposal-planning/SKILL.md`](../skills/research-proposal-planning/SKILL.md)
- **Data plan notes:** [`data-plan.md`](../skills/research-proposal-planning/references/data-plan.md)
- **Template it fills:** [`proposal-outline.md`](../skills/research-proposal-planning/assets/templates/proposal-outline.md)
- **Sample size and layout:** [`experimental-design`](experimental-design.md) · **Consent and privacy:** [`data-governance-ethics`](data-governance-ethics.md) · **The paper:** [`research-writing-reporting`](research-writing-reporting.md)

## 1. Idea to aims

> My idea: use public single-cell data to find out why some tumours resist a drug. Turn it into a question, say whether it is exploratory or confirmatory, and write two or three aims, each with a measurable output, a success criterion, the main risk and a fallback.

A good answer: a one-sentence question, an honest label of exploratory unless a prediction is stated, aims
whose success can be tested by someone else, and a fallback for each; it asks what data exist before promising them.

## 2. Is the schedule realistic

> Here is my plan: data access 6 weeks, quality control 2, alignment 3, statistics 4, then writing 5 weeks and figures 2 weeks in parallel with writing. I have 24 weeks. Does it fit if I add a 30 percent buffer?

A good answer: the critical path of 20 weeks (data access, quality control, alignment, statistics, writing),
a 26 week total with a 30 percent illustrative buffer, and the conclusion that it does not fit 24 weeks, with
options such as cutting scope or starting access earlier.

## 3. Check a data management plan

> Check this data management plan for missing elements and weak spots, and tell me what a keyword check cannot judge: <paste>

A good answer: each of the six elements marked covered or missing, warnings for placeholders, "upon request"
without a repository, no volume or no timeline, and a statement that the funder's own template is the reference.

## 4. What could go wrong

> List the risks in this project and give each a likelihood, an effect, an early warning and a response. Add two decision gates where I should stop or change course: <paste plan>

A good answer: risks specific to the plan, including data access and approval delays, each with an early
warning and a response, and gates tied to measurable results.

## 5. Who is an author

> Three of us are working on this project. How should we decide authorship and record who did what, and when should we have that conversation?

A good answer: early agreement, the venue's authorship criteria read first, a contribution vocabulary such
as the CRediT roles, and a note that the exact criteria must be checked at the source.

## 6. Thesis outline

> Outline a thesis chapter plan from these three aims: <paste aims>. Show how a failed aim changes the plan and where the data management and ethics sections go.

A good answer: chapters that map to aims, an explicit plan for a failed or negative result, a data
management and sharing section, and consent and access limits noted early.
